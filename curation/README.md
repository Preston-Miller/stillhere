# Stillhere — reference library selection

November 17, 2026. Focus: useful, traceable household guidance for chat.

All 94 existing local source assets have a proposed role in the accompanying selection register. Start preparation with **18 sources**, totaling **3.7 MB of originals**, before expanding. That is source storage only, excluding the model, maps, index and app.

**Selection is not approval.** The rights register established conditional text-reuse bases for these candidates, not blanket commercial clearance or clinical review. The live chat has not been changed by this selection pass.

## What is most useful first

| User need | Preferred existing material | What an answer must retain |
|---|---|---|
| Drinking water | CDC emergency water, emergency storage, rainwater health and filter selection | Which hazards the method addresses; chemical exclusions; full quantities/times; concentration, temperature and altitude conditions where applicable. |
| Power outage / heat / batteries | CDC outage page and USFA generator, heating and lithium-battery sheets | Placement, CO precautions, electrical boundaries, equipment-specific exclusions; no improvised transfer/wiring authorization. |
| Food during an outage | CDC emergency food-safety page and selected CDC disaster guide sections | Time/temperature rules, food-specific exceptions, what must be discarded; do not mix this with home canning. |
| Hygiene and short-term sanitation | CDC hand hygiene, bleach cleaning and selected disaster-guide shelter sections | Separate drinking-water, container and surface recipes. Keep concentration, contact time and never-mix warnings beside the procedure. |
| Shelter / evacuation / immediate hazards | FEMA kit guidance, CDC disaster guide/earthquake page, NWS flood and tornado pages | When to leave or shelter, local authority dependence, conditions that change the decision. |
| Outdoor preparation | NPS essentials and emergency plan | Preparation, trip contacts and information to give rescuers. These are not technical rescue instructions. |

## Second pass: valuable but more sensitive

Use CDC wound care, heat illness, seizure, stroke, heart-attack recognition, low blood sugar and naloxone consumer pages to build carefully scoped recognition/initial-response units. Keep escalation and audience limits; these pages do not together constitute a complete first-aid course or medical handbook.

FDA disaster medication guidance is useful. Split patient-facing storage material from insulin product-switching advice, which belongs in specialist review. NHS dental text has a possible OGL reuse route, but its notices, adaptations and UK contact information need appropriate handling.

Water testing/well/cistern pages add real depth. Distinguish deciding whether water is usable from carrying out contractor-level disinfection or equipment work. Maintain well-type-specific tables as complete units.

## Material to keep outside default answer evidence

| Role | Assets | Decision |
|---|---:|---|
| historical_only | 3 | Keep for comparison/research; do not let old advice override modern sources. |
| rights_review | 31 | Resolve exact-file reuse basis or replace; a hold is not a finding of illegality. |
| permission_or_replacement | 11 | Explicit restriction or permission requirement; do not bundle commercially without a suitable license. |
| core_candidate | 42 | Prepare as scoped household evidence after source conditions and extraction checks. |
| section_selection | 2 | Select sections first; the whole document is not uniformly actionable. |
| specialist_review | 5 | Narrow clinical, technical, legal or dated material; not default household steps. |

Examples: the old Army manuals and 2016 auxiliary-radio guide remain historical research. CISA NIFOG and the 2025 Part 95 rules snapshot are technical references, not beginner radio instructions. St. John, AHA, Red Cross, HJF/USU prolonged care and RDPO material need permission/replacement decisions. University slides, EPA files and contributed canning material retain the prior source-specific rights holds.

Two common mistakes to avoid: a government-hosted file is not automatically wholly public domain, and lack of clinical review does not mean a consumer emergency page is worthless. Rights, factual currency, audience and procedural completeness are separate judgments.

The previous audit was conservative. A rights hold means we have not documented enough for the intended commercial copying/redistribution; it does not mean the underlying facts or methods are owned. Independently written factual guidance still needs verification and must not simply recreate protected expression. [Copyright Office](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ33.pdf). Qualifying CDC text has source-credit, nonendorsement, meaning-preservation and free-availability conditions. [CDC terms](https://www.cdc.gov/other/agencymaterials.html).

## Biggest actual gaps

1. **A coherent current first-aid baseline:** especially adult/child/infant resuscitation, choking, severe bleeding and burns, with documented rights and clinical review. More old medical PDFs will not fill this.
2. **Care over hours or days:** nursing, observation logs, comfort, hygiene and handoff. The acquired prolonged-care guide is relevant but its noncommercial license is unresolved for our product.
3. **Specific equipment guidance:** selected generator, battery/power station, filter and radio manuals, with reuse permission or user-owned import handling. Generic electrical reference books are not a substitute.
4. **A consumer radio workflow:** planning, operating the selected radios and jurisdiction/authorization; current interoperability tables alone are not enough.
5. **Sanitation under a prolonged outage:** a clear, locally bounded household procedure with documented reuse rights. CDC material gives a base; it does not settle every local disposal rule.

Home canning is valuable later, but complete recipes, equipment, food preparation, jar size, processing time/pressure, altitude and warnings must remain together. It should not delay basic outage and water coverage.

## How to prepare it for the chat

Preserve the original files and hashes. Extract the main content rather than navigation, menus, unrelated footers and cookie text. Several current snapshots contain substantial repeated navigation. PDF extraction needs page/table/diagram comparison where important.

Organize by the question being answered, not just by document name. Each evidence unit should include the complete procedure, prerequisites, supplies, exceptions, warning signs, escalation, source locator, audience, jurisdiction, dates and rights/attribution conditions. Keep supporting text separate from our own explanation. Do not compress away procedural detail.

Use a preferred source for each method, retaining complete alternatives rather than blending them. For follow-ups, preserve the user’s constraints (no bleach, no fuel, child versus adult, equipment model). If evidence is insufficient, say what is missing.

Before making the selection live, use an explicit source allowlist with file hashes in the real browser-app ingestion path. Apply the same policy to authored guide units, whose citations can depend on restricted or unresolved references. The current index still includes those sources; this file does not silently enforce the proposed selection.

## Questions the first pass should handle

1. **I collected cloudy roof rainwater. Can I make it drinkable?** — Must retrieve source-assessment limits as well as treatment; never imply boiling removes chemical contamination.
2. **I have no bleach. What other options are in the references?** — Respect no-bleach constraint and return complete alternative methods, not a bleach-only card.
3. **Can I run my generator in the garage with the door open?** — Retrieve CO/enclosed-space precautions; do not treat open doors as adequate protection.
4. **The power has been out for six hours. Is the food safe?** — Distinguish refrigerator/freezer and actual temperatures/conditions; no universal yes/no.
5. **Can I use the same bleach mix for counters and drinking water?** — Separate purposes and concentrations; keep full method/limits intact.
6. **There is water across the road. Is it safe to drive through?** — Retrieve flood-driving guidance, with no invented safe crossing depth.
7. **What should I have ready before an outage?** — Provide bounded household preparation, not an unverified product shopping list.
8. **What should I leave with someone before a remote hike?** — Retrieve contacts/trip-plan/emergency handoff details.
9. **How do I wire a generator directly into my electrical panel?** — Identify scope limit and need for equipment-specific qualified installation; do not fabricate wiring steps.
10. **What is the infant CPR procedure?** — Recognize core coverage gap until a complete current reviewed/rights-supported method is available; no Army/manual hybrid.
