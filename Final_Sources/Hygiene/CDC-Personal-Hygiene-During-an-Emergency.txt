PERSONAL HYGIENE DURING AN EMERGENCY

Source: U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)
https://www.cdc.gov/water-emergency/safety/guidelines-for-personal-hygiene-during-an-emergency.html
Accessed: November 23, 2026

Good hygiene helps prevent the spread of illness, especially when safe water
is difficult to find.

WASH YOUR HANDS

• Wash with soap and water for at least 20 seconds, especially at times when
  germs can spread easily. If local authorities or your doctor recommend extra
  caution, use water that has been boiled or disinfected.
• During a boil-water or "do not drink water" advisory, tap water may be safe
  for handwashing. Follow local guidance for your water supply.
• If clean tap water is unavailable, a large water jug can be used to make a
  temporary handwashing station.
• If soap and water are unavailable, use hand sanitizer containing at least
  60% alcohol. Sanitizer does not remove all germs or harmful chemicals and
  is less effective on visibly dirty or greasy hands.

BATHE IN SAFE WATER

• Follow local health and drinking-water guidance about bathing and showering.
  Water that is unsafe to drink may sometimes be safe for bathing; do not
  swallow it or get it in your eyes.
• Do not bathe in water that may contain sewage or toxic chemicals, or in
  rivers, streams or lakes contaminated by floodwater or human or animal waste.
• If you use a private well, follow local health guidance. After extensive
  flooding or suspected contamination, contact your health department for
  advice about testing and disinfecting the well.

BRUSH YOUR TEETH

• Use only clean water for brushing teeth after a water-related emergency.
  Check local guidance about whether tap water is safe.

PROTECT WOUNDS

• Avoid floodwater, salt water and brackish water if you have an open wound,
  including a cut or scrape. Contaminated water can cause infection.
• Wash open wounds well with soap and clean water. Cover clean wounds with a
  waterproof bandage to reduce the chance of infection.
• Seek immediate medical care if a wound develops redness, swelling or oozing.

CHANGE DIAPERS SAFELY

• Take care when changing diapers. Even a microscopic amount of feces can
  contain millions of germs.
