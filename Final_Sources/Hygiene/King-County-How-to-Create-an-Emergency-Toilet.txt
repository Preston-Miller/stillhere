EMERGENCY TOILET AND SANITATION
Source: https://cd.kingcounty.gov/en/dept/dph/health-safety/safety-injury-prevention/emergency-preparedness/personal-preparedness/emergency-toilets
Accessed: November 23, 2026

When normal sewer service is disrupted, use an emergency toilet to contain
waste until a safe disposal option is available. The right disposal method
depends on the situation and local public health instructions.

USING A TOILET OR BUCKET

• Set a five-gallon bucket or other suitable container away from food and
  eating areas. An existing toilet bowl can also hold the liners.
• Line the container with two heavy-duty garbage bags. Put an absorbent
  material, such as cat litter, sawdust or fireplace ash, in the bottom.
• Keep the container covered between uses, and supervise young children.
• Set up soap, running water and paper towels nearby so people can wash their
  hands after using it. Do not flush the bags.

COLLECTING AND DISPOSING OF WASTE

• At the end of each day, securely tie the bags and place them somewhere
  protected, such as an outbuilding, until they can be disposed of safely.
• Follow local public health and waste-management directions. In King County,
  bagged waste may go in regular garbage during a declared emergency only if
  officials specifically announce that this is allowed.
• King County also describes disposal in a working sewer or septic system or
  burial on one's own property as possible options. Rules and conditions vary;
  confirm locally before choosing either method.

OUTDOOR OPTIONS

• Keep outdoor latrines and portable toilets at least 100 feet from lakes,
  streams and other surface waters, and at least 100 feet downhill or away
  from drinking-water sources, homes and campsites.
• King County says not to use a dug latrine in an urban location. Its guidance
  calls for a hole at least 3 feet deep, with the bottom at least 1 foot above
  the water table or hardpan; if the location cannot meet both conditions, it
  is unsuitable. Local rules may differ.
• Cover the latrine between uses. The county lists dirt, mulch, ash and lime as
  materials that can be added after use to control odors and pests.
• Portable toilets need regular pumping by a licensed contractor.
