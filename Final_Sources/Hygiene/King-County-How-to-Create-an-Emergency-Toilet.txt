HOW TO CREATE AN EMERGENCY TOILET

Source: Public Health — Seattle & King County, Washington
https://cd.kingcounty.gov/en/dept/dph/health-safety/safety-injury-prevention/emergency-preparedness/personal-preparedness/emergency-toilets
Accessed: November 23, 2026

If sewage systems are not functioning, a temporary toilet can help collect
human waste until normal service returns.

SET UP A TEMPORARY TOILET

1. Choose a toilet bowl, a five-gallon pail, or another suitably sized waste
   container. Put two heavy-duty plastic garbage bags inside as liners.
2. Put an absorbent material, such as cat litter, fireplace ash or sawdust,
   in the bottom of the bags.
3. Place the toilet away from areas where food is prepared or eaten. Keep it
   covered when not in use to keep out animals and insects and prevent injury.
4. Provide a nearby handwashing station with soap, running water and paper
   towels. Supervise small children using the toilet.

STORAGE AND DISPOSAL

• At the end of each day, tie the bags securely. Move them to a protected
  location, such as a garage, basement or outbuilding, until safe disposal is
  available.
• Follow instructions from local public health and waste authorities. King
  County says that, during a declared emergency, bagged waste may go into
  regular garbage only if a public announcement permits it.
• King County lists disposal into a properly functioning public sewer or septic
  system, or burial on one's own property, as possible options. Local rules and
  site conditions may differ; check with local authorities before disposal.
  Do not flush the plastic bags.

OUTDOOR LATRINES AND PORTABLE TOILETS

• Place outdoor latrines and portable toilets at least 100 feet from surface
  waters such as lakes, streams and rivers, and at least 100 feet downhill or
  away from a drinking-water source, home, apartment or campsite.
• Latrines are not appropriate in urban locations. King County says a latrine
  hole must be at least 3 feet deep, with its bottom at least 1 foot above the
  water table or hardpan. If a site cannot meet both requirements, it is not
  appropriate for a latrine.
• Cover a latrine between uses. The source recommends adding dirt, lime, mulch
  or ash after each use to reduce odors and deter insects and rodents.
• Portable toilets need regular pumping by a licensed contractor to prevent
  health hazards.

This is a formatted summary of King County guidance. Disposal requirements and
permitted sanitation methods may differ outside King County.
