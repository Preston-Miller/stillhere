SEVERE WEATHER SAFETY

Source: National Weather Service, Atlanta/Peachtree City
https://www.weather.gov/ffc/safety
Accessed: November 23, 2026

Severe weather can bring tornadoes, damaging wind, hail, lightning and flash
flooding. Use the section below that matches the immediate danger.

TORNADOES

Tornadoes can develop quickly. Take shelter immediately.

• Go to an underground shelter, basement or safe room.
• If none is available, go to a small interior room or hallway without windows
  on the lowest floor of a sturdy building.
• Leave a mobile home and go immediately to the nearest sturdy building or
  shelter.
• If caught outdoors without a basement or sturdy building nearby, get into a
  vehicle, buckle your seat belt and try to reach the closest sturdy shelter.
• If flying debris occurs while you are driving, pull over and park. As a last
  resort, choose one of these options:
  - Stay in the vehicle with your seat belt on. Keep your head below the
    windows and cover it with your hands and, if possible, a blanket.
  - If you can safely reach a place noticeably lower than the roadway, leave
    the vehicle and lie there, covering your head with your hands.

DAMAGING WIND AND HAIL

• For damaging wind, follow the same general shelter rules as for a tornado:
  enter a sturdy building and go to a basement or interior room on the lowest
  floor. If caught outside, seek a low-lying area.
• Large hail can break vehicle windows. If you cannot safely get inside, cover
  yourself with a blanket to help protect against broken glass.

LIGHTNING

• When you hear thunder, go indoors to a building.
• If you cannot reach a sturdy building, get into a hard-topped vehicle and
  close all the windows.
• Do not stand near tall objects such as trees, towers or utility poles.
• Do not use a corded phone.
• Keep away from electrical equipment, wiring and water pipes. Do not bathe,
  shower or use other plumbing during a thunderstorm.

FLASH FLOODING

• Move to higher ground. Avoid walking, driving or swimming in floodwater.
• Stay away from high water, storm drains, ditches, ravines and culverts.
  Moving water only six inches deep can knock a person off their feet.
• Do not allow children to play near storm drains.
• Never drive through a flooded roadway. Turn around and find another route.
