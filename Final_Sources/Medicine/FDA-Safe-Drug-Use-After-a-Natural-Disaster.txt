SAFE DRUG USE AFTER A NATURAL DISASTER

Source: U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA)
https://www.fda.gov/drugs/emergency-preparedness-drugs/safe-drug-use-after-natural-disaster
Accessed: November 20, 2026

This guide covers medicines affected by fire, flooding, unsafe water or loss
of refrigeration. After a disaster, check medicines for damage and improper
storage.

MEDICINES EXPOSED TO HEAT OR UNSAFE WATER

• High heat from a fire can reduce or destroy a medicine's effectiveness.
  Consider replacing medicines that may have been exposed to excessive heat.
• Medicines that have touched floodwater or potentially contaminated water
  should be discarded. This includes medicines in their original containers
  with screw-top caps, snap lids or droppers.

Exception for a medicine needed for a life-threatening condition:
If medicine exposed to fire or unsafe water is needed to treat a life-threatening
condition and a replacement is not readily available, FDA says it may be used
if it appears unchanged. Replace it as soon as possible. Medicine that is wet
or otherwise altered should not be used; discard it.

MEDICINES THAT NEED REFRIGERATION

Some medicines, including insulin and certain liquid antibiotics, require
refrigeration. If a medicine was not kept at its recommended temperature:

1. Check its storage instructions, if available.
2. Contact a pharmacist, health care provider or the manufacturer to find out
   whether it can still be used.
3. Medicines may lose potency when stored outside their recommended
   temperatures. Replace medicines that can no longer be used as soon as possible.

If a medicine is needed to sustain life and a replacement is not readily
available, FDA says it may be used until a new supply can be obtained.

FDA has separate guidance for insulin:
https://www.fda.gov/drugs/emergency-preparedness-drugs/information-regarding-insulin-storage-and-switching-between-products-emergency

MEDICINES THAT MUST BE MIXED WITH WATER

Use only purified or bottled water to mix a medicine that must be made into a
liquid. Do not use another liquid.

KEEPING MEDICINES READY

• Keep an up-to-date list of medicines, including each name, strength, purpose,
  dose and schedule. Include medical conditions, supply needs and allergies.
• Keep copies of prescriptions with emergency supplies.
• Know how much medicine you have. If pharmacy access may be disrupted, obtain
  early refills and ask a pharmacist or health care provider about an emergency
  supply and your state's emergency refill rules.
• If flooding or water damage is possible, put medicine bottles or packages in
  watertight containers and store them where floodwater is unlikely to reach.
• Plan how to keep refrigerated medicines cold during an outage. Have a cooler
  and ice or frozen gel packs available, and follow the medicine's storage label.
