STILLHERE — MEDICINE QUICK GUIDE
Updated 20 November 2026

This guide covers six common nonprescription medicines for short-term use.
It is not a complete medicine reference.

FIND A MEDICINE
  Pain or fever ........... Acetaminophen; ibuprofen
  Allergy symptoms ........ Cetirizine
  Diarrhea ................ Loperamide
  Heartburn ............... Calcium carbonate
  Minor itchy skin ........ Hydrocortisone cream

BEFORE USING A MEDICINE

• Read the Drug Facts label on the actual package. The doses below describe
  specific US products, not every product with the same ingredient. Strength,
  formulation, age directions, and daily limits can differ. If the package
  differs from this guide, follow the package and ask a pharmacist if unsure.
• Check the active ingredients in all medicines being taken. Cold, flu and
  combination products may contain the same medicine under another name.
• Ask a health professional before use during pregnancy or breastfeeding,
  for an infant, or when managing a serious condition or taking other drugs.
• Keep a written record of the medicine, strength, dose and time given. Store
  medicines as directed, in their original labeled containers, out of reach
  of children. An offline copy cannot alert you to updated warnings or recalls.
• For a suspected overdose, seek medical help or call US Poison Control at
  1-800-222-1222 immediately, even if the person seems well. For collapse,
  seizure, trouble breathing, or inability to wake, call 911 if available.

Poison emergencies: https://poisonhelp.hrsa.gov/what-you-can-do

ACETAMINOPHEN — PAIN AND FEVER

What it is for: Temporary relief of minor pain, such as headache, toothache,
muscle aches or menstrual cramps; also reduces fever.

Example product: Rising Pharma acetaminophen 325 mg film-coated tablets,
NDC 57237-346. The directions below apply only to this exact strength and
product label.

• Ages 12 and over: 2 tablets (650 mg) every 4–6 hours while symptoms last.
  Maximum: 10 tablets (3,250 mg) in 24 hours. Do not use longer than 10 days
  unless directed by a doctor.
• Ages 6–11: 1 tablet (325 mg) every 4–6 hours while symptoms last. Maximum:
  5 tablets (1,625 mg) in 24 hours. Do not use longer than 5 days unless
  directed by a doctor.
• Under age 6: Ask a doctor. Do not use these directions for children's liquids
  or other strengths.

Important warnings: Too much can cause severe liver damage. Do not combine
with any other medicine containing acetaminophen. Ask a doctor first if the
person has liver disease; ask a doctor or pharmacist if taking warfarin.
The risk of liver damage increases with three or more alcoholic drinks daily.

Possible serious effects: Allergic or serious skin reactions can occur; stop
using it and seek medical help for rash, blistering or swelling. An overdose
may cause liver damage even before symptoms appear.

Stop and seek advice if fever worsens or lasts more than 3 days, pain worsens,
or new symptoms appear. Source: Rising Pharma Drug Facts, updated October 2,
2026: https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=bd5e6804-4df4-4a65-92e4-faa3a0f7c005
FDA skin reaction warning: https://www.fda.gov/drugs/safe-use-over-counter-pain-relievers-and-fever-reducers/acetaminophen

IBUPROFEN — PAIN AND FEVER

What it is for: Temporary relief of minor pain, including muscle aches,
headaches, toothaches and menstrual cramps; also reduces fever. Ibuprofen is
an NSAID (a nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug).

Example product: Southern Sales & Services ibuprofen 200 mg film-coated
tablets, NDC 69822-693. These directions describe that label only.

• Ages 12 and over: 1 tablet (200 mg) every 4–6 hours while symptoms last.
  If one tablet does not relieve pain or fever, 2 tablets (400 mg) may be
  used. Do not exceed 6 tablets (1,200 mg) in 24 hours unless directed by a
  doctor. Use the smallest effective dose.
• Under age 12: Ask a doctor. Do not use these directions for children's
  formulations.

Important warnings: Can cause stomach bleeding. Ask a doctor before use if
age 60 or older, or if there is a history of ulcers, bleeding, kidney disease,
heart disease, high blood pressure, stroke or asthma. Ask about other
medicines, especially blood thinners, steroids, diuretics, heart-protective
aspirin or other NSAIDs such as naproxen. Do not use after a previous allergic
reaction to ibuprofen or another pain reliever, or around heart surgery.

Pregnancy: Ask a health professional before use if pregnant or breastfeeding.
FDA advises avoiding NSAIDs at 20 weeks of pregnancy or later unless a health
professional specifically directs their use.

Possible effects: Stomach upset may occur; the label suggests taking it with
food or milk if this happens. Stop and get urgent help for vomiting blood,
black or bloody stools, faintness, chest pain, trouble breathing, facial
swelling or signs of stroke. Stop and ask a doctor if pain lasts more than
10 days or fever lasts more than 3 days, or either worsens.

Sources: Southern Sales & Services Drug Facts:
https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=ec578861-5a50-5362-e053-2995a90ab54a
FDA pregnancy warning:
https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-and-availability/fda-recommends-avoiding-use-nsaids-pregnancy-20-weeks-or-later-because-they-can-result-low-amniotic

CETIRIZINE — ALLERGY SYMPTOMS

What it is for: An antihistamine for temporary relief of sneezing, runny nose,
itchy or watery eyes, and itchy nose or throat caused by hay fever or other
upper respiratory allergies. It is not emergency treatment for anaphylaxis.
For a severe allergic reaction, call 911 or the local emergency number and
use the person’s emergency epinephrine immediately if available. Do not give
anything by mouth if the person is having trouble breathing.

Example product: Major Pharmaceuticals cetirizine hydrochloride 10 mg
film-coated tablets, NDC 0904-7510. These directions describe that label.

• Ages 6–64: 1 tablet (10 mg) once daily. Maximum: 1 tablet in 24 hours.
• Ages 65 and over: Ask a doctor.
• Under age 6: Ask a doctor.
• Liver or kidney disease: Ask a doctor; a different dose may be needed.

Possible effects: Drowsiness can occur. Alcohol, sedatives and tranquilizers
can make it worse. Avoid alcohol and take care when driving or operating
machinery. Do not use if previously allergic to this product, its ingredients,
or an antihistamine containing hydroxyzine. If breastfeeding, this particular
label says use is not recommended; if pregnant, ask a health professional.

Source: Major Pharmaceuticals Drug Facts:
https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=71db132d-dca4-47ef-8560-c68ad2258933
MedlinePlus, anaphylaxis: https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000844.htm

LOPERAMIDE — DIARRHEA

What it is for: Temporary control of diarrhea, including travelers' diarrhea.
It does not replace fluids or treat dehydration.

Example product: Ohm Laboratories loperamide hydrochloride 2 mg caplets,
NDC 51660-123. These directions describe that product label.

• Ages 12 and over: Take 2 caplets (4 mg) after the first loose stool, then
  1 caplet (2 mg) after each later loose stool. Maximum: 4 caplets (8 mg) in
  24 hours. Never exceed this limit: overdose can cause dangerous heart
  rhythms or death.
• Ages 2–11: This label has different weight- and age-dependent directions.
  Ask a doctor or pharmacist and use the exact package directions; do not
  use the adult dose.
• Under age 2: Do not use.

Important warnings: Do not use if stool is bloody or black, or after an
allergic reaction to loperamide. Ask a doctor first if there is fever, mucus
in the stool, liver disease or a history of abnormal heart rhythm. Ask a
health professional about prescription medicines, pregnancy or breastfeeding.
Stop use and ask a doctor if diarrhea worsens, lasts more than 2 days, or
abdominal swelling develops.

Possible effects: Tiredness, dizziness or drowsiness. Take care when driving.
Drink fluids to replace losses. An oral rehydration solution can help replace
water and salts, especially when diarrhea is severe. Seek urgent help for
severe dehydration or inability to keep fluids down; children can become
dehydrated quickly.

CALCIUM CARBONATE — HEARTBURN AND ACID INDIGESTION

What it is for: An antacid for heartburn, acid indigestion and associated
upset stomach.

Example product: Spirit Pharmaceutical calcium carbonate 500 mg chewable
tablets, NDC 68210-5027. These directions describe this product's label;
other antacids, even at the same strength, can have different limits.

• Ages 12 and over: Chew 2–4 tablets as symptoms occur. Maximum: 14 tablets
  in 24 hours. If pregnant, the label maximum is 10 tablets in 24 hours;
  consult a health professional before use.
• Under age 12: No dose is provided on this label; ask a doctor.
• Do not take the maximum dose for more than 2 weeks without a doctor's
  advice. Ask a doctor if symptoms continue beyond 2 weeks.

Important warnings: Antacids can interact with prescription medicines; ask
a doctor or pharmacist before use if taking one. Seek urgent help for chest
pain with trouble breathing, sweating, or pain spreading to the arm or jaw;
do not assume it is heartburn.

Possible effects: Constipation or belching may occur. Ask a health professional
before use if you have kidney disease. This product contains calcium.

HYDROCORTISONE 1% CREAM — MINOR ITCHY SKIN

What it is for: Temporary relief of itching from minor irritation, including
insect bites, poison ivy, soaps and certain rashes.

Example product: Preferred Pharmaceuticals hydrocortisone cream 1%,
NDC 68788-8078. The following directions are for this cream's label.

• Skin irritation, ages 2 and over: Apply to the affected area no more than
  3–4 times daily. Under age 2: Ask a doctor.
• For external anal or genital itching, follow the separate product-label
  directions; ask a doctor for children under 12.

Important warnings: External use only; avoid the eyes. Do not use for diaper
rash or genital itching with vaginal discharge; consult a doctor. Do not put
it inside the rectum. If swallowed, contact Poison Control immediately.

Possible effects: Skin irritation or burning may occur. Stop and ask a doctor
if the condition worsens, persists longer than 7 days, clears up and returns
within a few days, or rectal bleeding occurs.
